Website Concepts — comparison, recommendation and sitemap

Two CDS prototypes (vista1 — Concept A "Clarity"; vista3 — Concept C "Plain Sight") reviewed against the live vista.com.my on the same day, with the same browser, on desktop and mobile: screenshots, user-experience and content comparison, technical measurements (Lighthouse, axe-core, network), the recommendation and what it rests on, and the complete sitemap proposed for the new site. The prototypes cover four routes each; the sitemap covers the whole estate.

How to read this tab. vista1 and vista3 are concepts built to show the user journey and the look and feel — they are not the finished website. The production site is built on Storyblok + Next.js (the same stack CDS runs for its other clients) and the pages, centres, doctors and prices shown are placeholders. The personalised journey on vista3 asks three quick questions — who the visit is for, where you are, what brought you here — and adapts the page: the right treatment, what is included, and the nearest centre. Visitors who just want to browse choose "Skip for now" once at the first step. For a live example of this pattern in production, see the CDS-built CCIS site on our development server.

Recommendation — build on vista3 (Plain Sight), take four things from vista1

Go with vista3 as the design and content base for the new vista.com.my. It is the direction that serves the patients the numbers say matter most, it is the only one that is trilingual, and it is the lightest and most accessible of the three by a wide margin. Take from vista1 the price-forward hero card, the richer booking form fields, the consultant directory with structured data, and the "three steps to clear vision" explainer. Neither prototype ships as code — the production build is Storyblok + Next.js as the roadmap already specifies.

Why vista3

  1. It is built for the people who enquire most. Cataract is the largest enquiry category (12,853 conversations in 12 months) and the caregiver (P04) and senior (P05) personas carry the biggest media line. Plain Sight's 13–14 px minimum type, 56 px targets, A+ text control, functional motion and "who is this for?" routing are designed for exactly those visitors. vista1's flowing motion and 12 px footers are designed for the young refractive buyer, who is one segment of three.
  2. It answers the language finding. 9% of users read Mandarin and 37 of 43 Mandarin pages were broken (F-06); BM is the first language on TikTok. vista3 has real /ms/ and /zh/ routes with native copy; vista1 has a switcher that leads nowhere. The CNY campaign on 25 Jan 2027 lands on the new site, so this is not optional.
  3. It is the performance and accessibility target the RFP scores. 475 KB and 14 requests against 2.5 MB / 182 requests today and 10.8 MB / 32 requests for vista1 on mobile; Lighthouse 98 / 93 / 96 / 100 on desktop and 75 / 93 / 96 / 100 on mobile; one axe violation type (contrast) against five on the live site. The roadmap commits to sub-1.5 s FCP and WCAG 2.2 AA — vista3 is the only candidate already near both.
  4. It carries the booking flow the CRM redesign needs. "Who is this for?" is the first step of the wizard, which is what lets Respond.io route a caregiver to a coordinator, a parent to the kids path and a refractive enquiry to the price answer — the mechanics behind moving the paid booking rate from 1.5% to ≥ 6%.
  5. It is the easiest to make compliant. Plain language, no superlatives, no testimonials, a facility-only trust block. One line ("free suitability assessment") has to go; vista1 is also clean; the live site is not ("Top #1", "painless", "25× precision").

What vista1 does better — and comes across

  • The hero card that puts "iLASIK from RM188/month, 0% instalment" beside the headline — the single most direct answer to the unanswered price question (0 of 5 in the chat sample).
  • The booking form's detail: preferred time slots, insurance panel field, "what happens next", "bring these with you", and the red-flag notice — all of which reduce coordinator work.
  • Consultants listed with name, qualifications, sub-specialty and centre, marked up as Physician schema, and MedicalProcedure + FAQPage schema on treatments — the AI-visibility and rich-result groundwork in the SEO scope.
  • The nine-service-line grid with "the assessment to book first" on each — it turns a treatment menu into a next action.

Why not the current site, and why not vista1

The live site fails the RFP's own acceptance criteria on structure (two H1s, none on branch pages, 74 duplicate location URLs), accessibility (31 axe violations on the home page, 2 of 93 images with alt text), consent (eight tags fire with no CMP) and claims (the page title itself is a prohibited superlative). vista1 is a strong design but as deployed it is the slowest of the three on mobile (LCP 35 s, 0.40 layout shift) because of unoptimised imagery, it is English-only, and its motion-heavy language suits one persona group. Those are fixable, which is why its best parts are being carried across rather than the whole.

What was reviewed and how

  • Live site vista.com.my — home, iLASIK, cataract, kids eye exam, locations, PJ The Curve branch, about, BM and ZH home. WordPress 7.1 + Elementor, NitroPack cache. Note that the crawl hit NitroPack's cached copy from a data-centre; the August audit measured 7.3 s load and 151 requests from a Malaysian consumer connection, so the live site's numbers here are its best case.
  • vista1.devteam-cds.tech — Concept A "Clarity" (curved transitions, gradients, circular imagery, scroll-linked motion). Routes: home, treatments, about, contact.
  • vista3.devteam-cds.tech — Concept C "Plain Sight" (warm neutrals, 19 px body, 56 px targets, functional motion). Routes: home, treatments, treatment detail, about, contact, plus /ms/ and /zh/ mirrors. As deployed it renders a blank page on every route (React error #310 — a useEffect placed after an early return in the personalisation modal). CDS patched the bundle locally to review it; the fix is one line and is item 1 in the defects list.
  • Method — headless Chromium 140 at 1440 × 900 and iPhone-class 390 × 844; each page scrolled end-to-end before capture so reveal animations fire; network, timing, DOM, heading, alt, schema, tracking and form counts collected per page; axe-core 4 run with WCAG 2.0/2.1/2.2 A and AA rules; Lighthouse 12 mobile and desktop presets on each home page (vista3 through the patched local mirror). All figures are single runs and should be read as indicative, not as a certified baseline.
  • Scope caveat — both prototypes carry placeholder prices, centres, doctors and photography (Pexels). They are judged on structure, content model, interaction and technical hygiene, not on the data they show.

What this means for the build

  • SPA is not the production shape. Both prototypes are client-rendered Vite bundles: a crawler, a slow phone or a WhatsApp link preview sees an empty page until 380 KB of JavaScript runs. The roadmap's Storyblok + Next.js with static generation produces the same screens as HTML, which is what the SEO/AEO/GEO scope and the sub-1.5 s FCP target require. The prototypes are the specification for what those pages contain and how they behave.
  • Image discipline is the difference between vista1 and vista3's scores. vista3's bundle is larger than vista1's, yet it loads a tenth of the bytes because its images are sized; the same rule (responsive sizes, WebP/AVIF, width and height set, lazy below the fold) has to be enforced in the CMS image pipeline rather than left to editors.
  • Contrast is the one accessibility defect common to all three — blue-on-blue price text and light captions. Fixing the token values once in the design system clears it everywhere.
  • Consent before tags. The live site fires GTM, two GA4 properties, Google Ads, two Meta pixels and Hotjar on load with no banner. Neither prototype has a CMP yet; the build adds one with server-side tagging so measurement (analysis F-11) is legal and complete from day one.
  • Language routing moves from WPML query strings to path prefixes (/ms/, /zh/) with hreflang and a translated URL map for the 43 Mandarin pages that are broken today.

Proposed sitemap for the new vista.com.my

The complete estate, not the four-route prototype. Nine top-level groups, about 95 English pages, mirrored under /ms/ and /zh/ with hreflang. Every treatment page uses one template (what it is, who it is for, price range and instalment, day of procedure, recovery in days, FAQs, doctors who perform it, book), every centre page one template (hours, parking, doctors, services, directions, Google profile link, book), so the 16 branches and 20-odd treatments are data, not hand-built pages. Current URLs redirect one-to-one into this structure at go-live (22 Jan 2027).

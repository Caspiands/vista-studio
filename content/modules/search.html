01Where search stands today

The only SEO measure Vista receives is a count of keywords in the top 3 / top 10 / top 100. That count went up while the commercial keywords went down, because most of the tracked terms are brand names or near-zero volume. Underneath, the site structure is working against the network: duplicated centre pages, a sitemap that misses two years of content, three languages on broken URLs, and none of the medical entity signals that answer engines and AI assistants look for.

02SEO, AEO and GEO in plain terms

Three ways a patient finds Vista before they ever visit the site. They share one content engine and one technical foundation; what differs is where the answer appears and what earns it.

SEO · search engine optimisation

Being found on Google

A patient types "cataract surgery" or "eye specialist Ipoh" and Vista's page appears in the results, and in the map pack for the nearest centre.

  • What earns it: a page for every service, every centre and every language; a fast, crawlable site; content that answers the question; other sites linking to Vista.
  • How it is measured: organic visitors and enquiries, keywords on page 1, centre × service pairs in the local top 3.
AEO · answer engine optimisation

Being the answer, not just a link

Google increasingly answers directly — AI Overviews, featured snippets, "People also ask", Knowledge Panels. AEO makes Vista's content the one those answers are built from.

  • What earns it: question-led pages with short, direct answers; FAQ and medical structured data; doctor profiles as recognised entities; consistent facts everywhere.
  • How it is measured: questions where Vista appears in the answer box, Knowledge Panel presence, FAQ rich results.
GEO · generative engine optimisation

Being cited by AI assistants

Patients ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude or Copilot "where should I get LASIK in KL?". GEO is about Vista being named, described correctly and linked when they do.

  • What earns it: citation-ready content (who wrote it, who reviewed it, when, sources), clear facts about Vista on the sites AI reads, a machine-readable summary (llms.txt), consistent third-party mentions.
  • How it is measured: a fixed set of patient prompts run monthly on five assistants: is Vista mentioned, cited, described correctly?

04Keyword and page plan

The commercial head terms Vista must own, the pillar and audience each one serves, and the page on the new site that is built to win it. Volumes are agency-reported monthly averages for Malaysia where they exist; every other term is sized in the Day-30 audit, when the full set is agreed with Vista. Filter by track or language; select a heading to sort.

Language rule: EN and ZH for refractive and cataract (where the searches are); BM first for kids, dry eye and the northern and east-coast centres; every service page exists in all three languages with hreflang so Google shows the right one.

05Local search — the 16 centres

"Eye specialist near me" and "<city> eye specialist" are the highest-intent searches Vista receives and the ones the current structure handles worst: 101 location URLs for 16 centres, no H1 on any of them, 9 centre pages under 110 words, and 13 Google listings for 16 centres with a duplicate HQ. Local is its own track because it is where a search turns into a visit.

What changes on the website

  • One page per centre, per language — 16 canonical pages with address, hours, parking, doctors on site, services offered, phone in one format, map and a booking button that pre-selects the centre. The 74 duplicate and legacy URLs redirect to them.
  • LocalBusiness / MedicalClinic markup on every centre page, matching the Google listing exactly (name, address, phone, hours).
  • Centre × service pages for the highest-value pairs (e.g. cataract surgery Ipoh, LASIK Penang, kids eye check Johor Bahru) — built from the sitemap's centre and service templates, not hand-written.
  • Personalised journey feeds local: a visitor who says "Penang" is shown the Penang centre and its doctors, and that page is what Google indexes for Penang searches.

What changes on Google Business Profile

  • 16 listings, verified and owned by Vista — the three missing centres added, the duplicate HQ listing merged, keyword-stuffed names replaced with the real centre name (Google's rules), categories set to Eye care center / Ophthalmologist / LASIK surgeon.
  • One post per centre per month (already in the content plan) plus services, Q&A and photos kept current from the CMS.
  • Review workflow — a request after the one-month review, routed by centre; replies within two business days; reviews about the facility only, per MAB 3/2023.
  • Tracking — calls, direction requests and website clicks per listing into the Command Centre, so each centre sees its own numbers.

Priority centre × service pairs

Tracked in the local pack (top 3) from Month 4. Targets: ≥ 8 pairs by Month 6, ≥ 16 by Month 12. The list is confirmed with Vista at the Day-45 roadmap approval; the pairs below follow where enquiries and searches concentrate today.

06Answer engines — being the answer Google shows

Google now answers many eye-care questions on the results page itself. Vista is absent from those answers today because the site has no FAQ blocks worth quoting, no medical structured data and no doctor entities. The fix is editorial as much as technical: every service page carries the questions patients ask in chat, answered in two sentences, marked up so Google can lift them.

Content pattern for every service page

  • Question-led sub-headings in the patient's words ("Am I suitable for LASIK?", "How many days off work?")
  • A direct 40–60 word answer first, detail after
  • An FAQ block of 5–8 questions taken from the Respond.io chat sample
  • Approved price range where MAB allows it, instalment terms, what is included
  • Reviewed-by line naming the ophthalmologist, with date

Structured data added

  • MedicalOrganization for Vista; MedicalClinic per centre
  • Physician per doctor — name, qualifications, sub-specialty, centre (facts only, no skill claims)
  • MedicalProcedure per treatment; FAQPage per service; BreadcrumbList
  • Consistent across the three languages; validated in CI before every release

Entity work

  • Knowledge Panel claimed and corrected (name, logo, description, sameAs links)
  • 20 doctor profiles as individual URLs (today: one shared page, empty alt text)
  • Consistent name/address/phone on every directory that Google and AI read
  • "People also ask" coverage tracked per service from Month 4

07AI assistants — being cited by ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude and Copilot

Visits referred by AI assistants grew from 7 to 134 a month between May and August 2026 with an 89–92% engagement rate — patients who arrive this way already trust the recommendation. Nobody at Vista measures it. GEO makes it measurable from Day 30 and then works on the things assistants reward: clear facts, credible authorship, and being mentioned consistently on the sources they read.

How it is measured

  • Prompt library — ~60 patient prompts in EN, BM and ZH ("best LASIK clinic in Kuala Lumpur", "cataract surgery cost Malaysia", "children's myopia control Penang"), version-controlled so results are comparable month to month.
  • Five engines — ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Claude via API; Copilot checked manually. Run monthly; benchmarked quarterly against three competitors.
  • Three scores per prompt — mentioned (Vista named), cited (Vista linked), accurate (services, centres and prices described correctly). Reported as raw counts, not a blended index.
  • Traffic — the GA4 "AI Assistant" channel tidied so chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, gemini and copilot are grouped once, and enquiries from that channel tracked in the ledger.

What earns citations

  • Citation-ready articles — named author, named reviewing ophthalmologist, publish and review dates, references to clinical sources, a one-paragraph summary at the top. Assistants quote pages that show their sources.
  • A facts page and llms.txt — one machine-readable page listing what Vista is, its 16 centres, services, doctors and accreditations, so an assistant does not guess.
  • Consistent third-party presence — directories, hospital and insurer panels, press, association listings all saying the same thing; corrections requested where they do not.
  • Original data — Vista's own figures (procedures a year, centres, years operating, patient-reported outcomes where approved) are what assistants cite over generic advice.
  • Compliance — the same MAB 3/2023 rules apply: no superlatives, no outcome guarantees, no practitioner-skill claims in anything written for assistants to quote.

08Technical foundation — built into the new website

Most of Vista's search problems are structural, so most of the fix ships with the website in Month 3 rather than as a separate SEO project. The concepts in the Website tab and the production stack in the Roadmap tab already carry these requirements; this is the search view of them.

Migration safeguard: a full crawl of both current sites is taken before launch; every URL with traffic or a ranking is mapped to its new page; rankings and organic sessions are compared at Week 1, Week 4 and Week 12 after launch, and any page that loses more than 20% is investigated within five days.

09Content engine — four medically reviewed articles a month

Content today arrives in batches (24 posts in one April, then nothing) and lives on a second domain nobody links to. The plan is a steady four articles a month on the main site, each planned from the keyword map, written for a persona, reviewed by a named ophthalmologist and published in the languages where the demand is.

What gets written

  • Condition guides (Know Your Eyes) — cataract, myopia, presbyopia, glaucoma, dry eye: symptoms, when to get checked, what a check involves
  • Decision guides (Weigh Your Options) — LASIK vs CLEAR PRO vs ICL, lens choices for cataract, recovery in days, paying for it
  • Caregiver guides (Every Age, Every Eye) — taking a parent for cataract surgery, a 12-month myopia plan for a child
  • Centre and doctor content (Inside Vista) — what each centre offers, the team, the equipment

How it is produced

  • Rolling calendar at least one month ahead, agreed at the monthly meeting
  • Brief → draft → clinical review (named Vista reviewer, 5-business-day SLA) → compliance check → translation → publish
  • Medical review workflow lives inside the CMS, so the reviewer signs off on the page itself
  • Quarterly refresh of the ten best-performing pages; quarterly contributory article for press or partners

Targets

  • First 4 articles published in Month 2; 16 by Month 4; 24 by Month 6; 52 by Month 12
  • ≥ 10% of articles in Google's top 10 for their target term by Month 12
  • Every article in EN; BM and ZH where the keyword map shows demand (kids, cataract, dry eye first)
  • All 2025–2026 posts migrated, de-duplicated and in the sitemap by Month 5; vistanow.com.my redirected

11Tools and technologies

Every tool is either Vista-owned or vendor-neutral, so the data and the history stay with Vista whichever agency runs the work. Subscriptions run for the full 12 months and are part of the retainer commercials — nothing here is an add-on charge. Where two products do the same job, the choice is made at the Day-30 audit on Vista's existing licences and on price.

12KPIs and reporting

The ranking count Vista receives today can rise while the business falls. From Month 2 the search report leads with visitors and enquiries and shows rankings only for the agreed commercial set. Every figure carries a confidence label in the Command Centre: verified, modelled or estimated.

The monthly search report

  • Organic sessions, enquiries and booked appointments from organic, by language and by centre — against last month and the post-launch baseline
  • Commercial keyword set: position, change, page that ranks (no brand-term padding)
  • Local pack: centre × service pairs in the top 3; GBP calls, directions and website clicks per centre
  • AEO: answer-box and "People also ask" appearances per service; rich-result status
  • GEO: prompt-library results — mentioned / cited / accurate per engine; AI-referred sessions and enquiries
  • Content published, reviewed and pending; technical health (crawl errors, Core Web Vitals, index coverage)
  • What CDS is doing next month and what it needs from Vista

Gates and checkpoints

  • Day 30 — audit, keyword/entity map and AI-visibility baseline delivered
  • Day 45 — 12-month search roadmap and the commercial keyword set approved by Vista
  • Go-live (Month 3) — pre-launch crawl frozen; redirect map verified; structured data validated
  • Month 4 — post-launch baseline locked; floors become binding from Month 5
  • Month 6 — KPI checkpoint: ≥ 50% commercial keywords on page 1, ≥ 8 local pairs, Vista cited on ≥ 3 engines
  • Month 12 — ≥ 60% on page 1, ≥ 16 local pairs, ≥ 5 engines, organic sessions +40%, 52 articles
